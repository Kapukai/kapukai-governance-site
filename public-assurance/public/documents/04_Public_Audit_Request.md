# Requested audit response and privacy safeguards

## Proposed method

Please define the covered population, exclusions and sampling method. Examine both written policies and their operation. Include decisions upheld, changed and unresolved. Where authorized and feasible, use population-wide tests for specified record defects. Publish limitations and do not characterize selected complaints as representative of all cases.

## Requested response

For each selected control, please identify jurisdiction; existing supporting records; evidence status; responsible office; whether further examination is planned; and any reason for declining. Suggested statuses are demonstrated, partial, not demonstrated, not applicable and withheld. Not demonstrated does not itself establish misconduct.

Please identify the office responsible for coordinating correction, the authority of each participating recipient, and the process for resolving refusal or disagreement. If the matter is outside your jurisdiction, identify an appropriate recipient where known; do not forward sensitive information not supplied or authorized by this request.

## Privacy and transparency

I seek institutional accountability without unnecessary family disclosure. For responsive records subject to public disclosure, release reasonably segregable nonexempt portions and identify applicable withholding grounds as required by law. Where public access is restricted, identify whether a legally authorized independent reviewer can examine the underlying material and publish protected findings. This is not consent to release personal information. [S7, S10]

## Routing guide

Use an oversight body with actual jurisdiction over the named program. For HHS programs, HHS OIG describes its complaint intake and anonymous options. For GAO work, a congressional request is one established route; a private request does not command a national audit. State auditors, inspectors general and ombuds offices have different powers and intake rules. Confirm the route rather than sending the same form indiscriminately. [S8, S9]

This form asks federal oversight questions when federal programs are involved. It is not a federal-question complaint under 28 USC 1331 and does not create standing or a cause of action. [S6]
