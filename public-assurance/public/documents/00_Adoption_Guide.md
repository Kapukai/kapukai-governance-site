# Public Systems Assurance Founding Package

Prepared for Kapukai Governance Lab • Discussion draft 0.1 • 27 September 2026

This package proposes independent technical review of consequential public decision systems, safe access for affected people, and verified correction. It supplies six working documents and a practical adoption route. It contains no findings about a named person, agency or case.

The immediate recommendation is to test a narrow correction workflow through a voluntary pilot, publish the method and limitations, and seek an authorized sponsor for implementation. A literal fourth constitutional branch is a separate constitutional project, not a power created by this package.

## Documents and intended users

1. Founding charter — for the founding coalition and participating institutions.
2. Model enabling legislation — for legislative counsel and potential sponsors. This is a federal discussion draft with a bounded executive-branch scope, not filing-ready legislation for all jurisdictions.
3. Technical audit standard — for engineers, auditors, domain specialists and affected-person representatives.
4. Public systems audit request — for anyone seeking review without including individual case details.
5. Protected individual review request — for use only after a competent recipient and protected intake procedure are established.
6. Correction and closure record — for the correction coordinator, record custodians, decision-makers and independent verifier.

## What the package can do now

A voluntary institution can adopt the standard by written agreement, provide records it may lawfully share, test the forms, and commit to a response process. Kapukai can publish a proposal, convene reviewers and report evidence-based pilot results. No government endorsement, compulsory access, legal representation or certification is implied.

## What requires additional authority

Compulsory records access, legally enforceable response deadlines, sanctions, public funding and changes to legal remedies require an appropriate legal instrument. A private standard does not confer jurisdiction, waive confidentiality, override court orders, pause deadlines or create a right to damages. Federal legislation cannot simply place all state courts under a new technical office. [S5–S8]

## Document status and public use

All SHALL statements are proposed requirements. In a pilot, they bind participants only to the extent lawfully adopted by agreement. In legislation, they take effect only if enacted and applicable. Edit the templates in Word or use the separate Markdown source files. They are not electronic intake systems and do not transmit information.

# How comparable institutions gained adoption

Historical comparison identifies useful mechanisms; it does not establish that this proposal has already earned consensus or legal authority.

## GAO in 1921

Congress established the General Accounting Office through the Budget and Accounting Act of 1921 to strengthen oversight of federal expenditure. The lesson is to define a public function, confer a mandate and provide institutional resources. Drafting a charter alone did not create GAO. [S1]

## NTSB independence in 1974

Congress reestablished the NTSB outside the Department of Transportation because transportation investigations could expose deficiencies in the systems DOT operated or regulated. Its history provides a concrete precedent for separating investigation from the institution examined. This does not make its transportation jurisdiction transferable to family courts or other domains. [S2]

## NIST Cybersecurity Framework in 2014

Following Executive Order 13636, NIST worked with stakeholders to develop a voluntary risk-based framework using existing standards, guidelines and practices. It used workshops and public input and continued as a convener. The applicable lesson is to publish a usable common language, test it, invite criticism and revise it rather than claim consensus in advance. [S3]

## ISO consensus development

ISO describes standards as voluntary agreements developed through an open process in which stakeholder views are considered. The transferable method is balanced participation, recorded comment resolution and version control. Kapukai cannot label this draft an ISO standard or imply ISO certification. [S4]

## Recommended sequence

Define one measurable defect → recruit a balanced working group → publish a draft → run a lawful pilot → publish results and disagreements → revise → obtain a formal adoption instrument → verify implementation.

This sequence is a recommendation drawn from the examples, not a universal historical law. Adoption depends on credible evidence, resources, institutional incentives and political support.

## Initial pilot question

When an authoritative source record is corrected, can the participating institution identify dependent records and decisions, reassess them and demonstrate completion without avoidable disclosure? Begin with synthetic records, then a small authorized retrospective sample. Include cases where no downstream change is warranted. Do not experiment on live custody, dispatch or emergency decisions.

# Adoption plan and invitation

## First ninety days

Days 1–15 — Convene a working group covering systems engineering, legal procedure, privacy and security, frontline operations, independent audit, and affected adults and child representation. Publish members, qualifications, funding and conflicts where safe; protect participants whose identity creates a risk. Pay affected-person advisers where funding permits. No constituency controls a majority of the standards committee.

Days 16–30 — Publish draft 0.1 and open a comment period. Record each substantive comment, proposed change, disposition and reason. Provide accessible and nontechnical participation options. Obtain legal and security review before receiving personal records. Recruit one willing institutional partner and one reviewer independent of its delivery team.

Days 31–60 — Execute a pilot agreement defining the process, records, lawful access, retention, responsibilities, costs and exit arrangements. Test synthetic failure scenarios first. Review a small authorized retrospective sample using predeclared selection criteria. Record failures to respond and records unavailable; do not convert them automatically into findings of wrongdoing.

Days 61–90 — Publish a privacy-reviewed pilot report with denominators, uncertainty, costs, disagreements and unresolved issues. Hold a review workshop, issue draft 0.2 with a change log, and approach an appropriate legislative or administrative sponsor with the tested proposal.

## Adoption gates

Proceed to a live pilot only after an identified authority approves access and the team demonstrates safe handling of test records. Proceed to expansion only after an independent reviewer can reproduce key findings, material security defects are addressed, and staffing can meet the response commitments. Set numerical success thresholds before the pilot; report changes to those thresholds.

Measure dependency identification, review completion, elapsed time, unsupported closure, correction acknowledgment, privacy incidents, false-positive defect findings and reviewer disagreement. Do not reward a high allegation count, a high defect count or a low reversal rate. Measure both unnecessary action and missed protective action.

## How to obtain adoption

Ask each audience for a bounded commitment: professional societies for technical reviewers; family and disability groups for usability review; an institution for one lawful pilot; an independent research group for evaluation; a sponsor for legislative drafting. Offer a versioned standard and reusable evidence schema without requiring purchase of Kapukai services. Separate consulting revenue from conformity decisions.

Suggested invitation: We invite your organization to review or pilot a common method for tracing consequential decisions to evidence and verifying correction. The first pilot addresses one existing correction workflow. Participation does not endorse claims about individual cases. Please identify a technical reviewer, an affected-person representative or a pilot contact, and disclose relevant conflicts. We will agree scope, privacy controls, resources and publication terms before receiving case information.

# Presidents and institutional reform

Presidents have helped establish oversight institutions and common standards. Their actions ordinarily depended on legal authority, congressional participation, funding and implementation by public servants. Creating an institution after election does not explain how that president first won office.

## Herbert Hoover before the presidency

Hoover trained and worked as a mining engineer, organized large relief efforts and served as Secretary of Commerce from 1921 to 1928. The Hoover Presidential Library links his administrative reputation and Commerce service to his 1928 candidacy. This is a relevant example of technical and organizational work contributing to political standing; it is not proof that an engineering oversight proposal is a reliable route to the presidency. His later presidential record also demonstrates that technical credentials do not guarantee successful governance. [S11]

## Richard Nixon and EPA

Nixon proposed Reorganization Plan No. 3 in 1970; EPA began operating on 2 December 1970 after the applicable statutory process. This consolidated previously dispersed environmental responsibilities. He was already president. The lesson is to define the gap and use an authorized mechanism to reorganize functions, not to assume every new office can be created by executive order. [S12]

## Jimmy Carter and inspectors general

Carter signed the bipartisan Inspector General Act on 12 October 1978, establishing twelve presidentially appointed inspectors general. This is particularly relevant to independent audit and investigation. It was legislation enacted during his presidency, not an institution he created to obtain the presidency. [S13]

## Barack Obama and NIST

Obama issued Executive Order 13636 in February 2013, directing development of a voluntary cybersecurity framework. NIST convened stakeholders and published version 1.0 in 2014. This illustrates presidential sponsorship of a technical standard within existing governmental structures, after election. [S3]

## Implication for this proposal

A president could be a sponsor of a lawfully authorized initiative. A lawmaker, professional body, state institution or coalition can also initiate development. The practical next milestone is a credible pilot and adoption partner, not a presidential campaign. No electoral success prediction follows from these historical examples.

## Before introducing the bill

Legislative counsel must review appointments and removal, executive supervision, appropriations, records-access conflicts, classification and privileges, federalism, judicial review, exhaustion, venue and sovereign-immunity language. Cost estimation, agency consultation and implementation staffing remain open work. No all-jurisdiction legal validity or likelihood of litigation success is claimed.

## Before sending either request

Confirm the recipient, its jurisdiction, intake requirements, confidentiality limits and safe reply method. Do not invent an official case number or submit a completed form as if the proposed Office already exists. No request in this package automatically suspends an order or extends a filing deadline.
