# Public Systems Assurance and Correction Act

PSA L01 • Federal legislative discussion draft 0.1 • Not enacted law

This draft establishes an executive-branch assurance office for specified federal administrative systems. It does not establish a fourth constitutional branch, review state-court judgments, or automatically regulate all state programs. All deadlines below are proposed. Legislative counsel must resolve the legal and fiscal items listed in the adoption guide before introduction.

## Section 1 Short title and purposes

This Act may be cited as the Public Systems Assurance and Correction Act. Its purposes are to provide independent technical examination of covered decision systems, accessible and protected review requests, reasoned institutional responses and verification of required correction.

## Section 2 Definitions

Covered agency means a federal executive department or agency designated by this Act or by regulations issued under section 11 within the Act's scope. Congress, federal courts, state and tribal governments are not covered agencies under this draft.

Covered system means a covered agency's administrative process, including a contractor-operated component, that materially supports a decision affecting liberty, family relationships, bodily autonomy, control of property or access to essential services. Contractor obligations apply within the relevant federal contract and lawful access terms.

Material assertion means a factual representation on which a covered decision substantially depends. Correction includes amendment, annotation, withdrawal or a reasoned determination following review. It does not require erasure of an accurate historical record.

Technical finding means a documented conclusion about system operation or conformity with an identified requirement, including evidentiary limits. It does not itself determine legal liability. Independent reviewer means a qualified person free from a material conflict concerning the reviewed function, appointed through recorded conflict screening.

## Section 3 Establishment and leadership

There is established in the executive branch a Public Systems Assurance Office. The Director shall be appointed by the President with the advice and consent of the Senate and shall be removable by the President. Organizational separation from audited agencies shall be maintained, consistent with lawful executive supervision and congressional oversight.

The Director shall appoint qualified technical, legal, privacy and domain personnel and establish an advisory council including affected-person representation. The council is advisory and shall exercise no governmental enforcement power. Any applicable federal advisory-committee requirements shall be observed.

The Office shall maintain separate audit and delivery functions. Neither an audited agency nor a contractor may select the reviewer for its own engagement, condition reviewer compensation on findings or veto publication. Legitimate classification, privilege and disclosure disputes shall be handled under section 5.

# Audit authority and protected access

## Section 4 Functions and intake

The Office shall accept systemic requests without individual case identifiers, publish risk-based audit priorities, and examine covered systems on its own initiative or upon referral within its jurisdiction. A request creates no entitlement to a full audit. The Office shall publish criteria for acceptance, consolidation, referral and closure.

Where a safe reply route exists, the Office shall acknowledge receipt within 10 business days and ordinarily provide a jurisdiction and triage determination within 30 calendar days. Credible reports of imminent serious harm shall receive expedited screening no later than the next business day after receipt during an operating intake period. Intake is not an emergency service and does not substitute for immediate protective response.

An individual review shall begin only after the requester receives a notice explaining the Office's powers, confidentiality limits, retention practices, alternatives and effect on existing proceedings. These requests shall not toll other deadlines or suspend orders.

## Section 5 Records and examination powers

Covered agencies shall furnish information reasonably necessary for an authorized audit, subject to applicable restrictions concerning privacy, classification, privilege, sealed records and specially protected information. A withholding agency shall identify its legal basis and provide a non-sensitive explanation sufficient for review where lawful. The Office shall seek lawful accommodation, minimization or secure examination before concluding that access is unavailable.

For records within the Act's lawful reach, the Director may issue an administrative subpoena stating scope, relevance and a reasonable response period. A recipient may object within 20 calendar days or a shorter period ordered by a court for good cause. The Office may petition an appropriate federal district court to enforce the subpoena. The court shall protect privileged material, personal safety and recipients from undue burden. This section does not override a separate statutory prohibition on disclosure.

The Office shall preserve originals or reliable copies as authorized, document provenance and provide a factual-response opportunity before publishing adverse institutional findings. A response is not a publication veto. Classified and protected findings shall follow authorized reporting channels.

## Section 6 Privacy and representation

Public reports shall disclose reasonably segregable, lawfully releasable institutional findings while protecting personal information and accounting for reidentification risk. Access under this Act is not permission for public release. Consent from one person shall not waive another person's rights or a court's sealing requirements.

The Office shall establish access controls, retention limits, incident response, secure channels and alternatives to public identification. Appropriate child and disability representation shall be provided through authorized arrangements. The Office shall not create an unrestricted national repository of family case files.

# Required responses and corrective action

## Section 7 Findings and agency response

Each final finding shall identify the applicable requirement, evidence, uncertainty, institutional response and corrective recommendation. A covered agency shall respond within 30 calendar days by accepting, contesting or requesting clarification of each finding, with reasons and supporting information. One extension of up to 30 days may be granted for recorded good cause; urgent risks require an interim response.

For an accepted defect or a defect sustained through the applicable review process, the agency shall designate a correction coordinator and issue a plan identifying tasks, lawful decision-makers, dependencies, interim safeguards, deadlines and verification evidence. The Office may issue a compliance notice requiring fulfillment of procedural duties expressly imposed by this Act. It may not adjudicate parentage, custody, guilt, clinical necessity or entitlement to benefits merely through a technical finding.

Where the Office identifies an imminent serious risk, it shall promptly notify the official with lawful protective authority and request a reasoned interim response. Nothing here creates a general power for an engineer to countermand judicial or emergency orders.

## Section 8 Correction and dependency review

Covered agencies shall maintain a proportionate means of identifying material record and decision dependencies. When a supporting assertion is corrected, withdrawn or materially qualified, the source custodian shall notify authorized recipients, and each recipient shall reassess affected records and decisions within a documented risk-based period.

Each recipient shall record whether the change requires amendment, suspension, termination, reaffirmation or no action, with its authority and reasons. Correction of the source alone shall not constitute closure while identified downstream duties remain incomplete. Previously disclosed sensitive information shall prompt containment and mitigation appropriate to the circumstances; recovery of every copy shall not be promised.

## Section 9 Enforcement and review

The Office shall publish aggregate overdue-action reports and refer persistent failures to the relevant agency head, inspector general and congressional oversight committees, subject to confidentiality limits. An unresolved technical dispute shall receive review by personnel who did not make the initial finding.

A person adversely affected by failure of a covered agency or the Office to perform a discrete mandatory duty under this Act may seek prospective declaratory or injunctive relief in an otherwise proper federal district court after requesting performance and allowing 30 days for response. A court may excuse that waiting period upon a showing of imminent irreparable harm. The United States waives sovereign immunity solely for that nonmonetary relief under this section. Article III standing and other jurisdictional requirements remain applicable.

This Act creates no damages remedy and no authority for collateral review of state judgments. It does not restrict otherwise available rights or remedies. Legislative counsel shall reconcile this section with existing review schemes, venue rules, exhaustion requirements and the Administrative Procedure Act before introduction.

# Implementation and statutory limits

## Section 10 Oversight of the Office

The Office shall publish funding, audit selection criteria, reviewer qualification standards and conflict procedures. It shall maintain independent reconsideration of technical findings and undergo an external performance and privacy audit at least every two years. The audited program shall not select that external auditor. Corrected Office findings shall be versioned and communicated to authorized recipients.

No payment, promotion or performance measure shall reward a specified number of adverse findings, allegations, detentions or favorable institutional conclusions. Evaluation shall include accuracy, timeliness, burden, equitable access, privacy and implementation of correction.

## Section 11 Standards and initial implementation

Within 180 days after appropriated funds become available, the Director shall publish a proposed implementation standard with at least 60 days for public comment. Adoption shall include reasoned resolution of significant comments, domain profiles and an assessment of staffing, cost and privacy impact. Within one year, the Office shall initiate limited pilots using synthetic records before authorized real records.

Initial federal profiles shall be selected by published risk criteria and operational feasibility. Procurement terms for future or modified contracts may incorporate applicable controls to the extent authorized by procurement law. Existing contracts shall not be retroactively rewritten by this standard alone.

State, territorial and tribal adoption may proceed through separately authorized agreements or legislation. Any grant conditions must be expressly authorized, clear, related to the funded program and constitutionally permissible. No authority over state or tribal courts is implied. Military, intelligence and national-security systems require specific access and jurisdiction provisions before inclusion.

## Section 12 Resources and evaluation

There are authorized to be appropriated such sums as Congress determines necessary to carry out this Act. Before seeking an appropriation, the proposed sponsor shall obtain a cost estimate covering intake, legal review, security, technical staffing, representation access, appeals and independent evaluation.

The Office shall publish an annual report on completed audits, pending requests, resource limitations, substantiated defects, contested findings, completed corrections, privacy incidents and review outcomes. Counts shall use defined denominators and avoid identifying small groups. Congress should commission an independent implementation evaluation after three years.

## Section 13 Construction and effective dates

This Act shall not be construed to create a fourth constitutional branch, confer criminal prosecutorial authority, presume misconduct from a missing record, or make every financial relationship a disqualifying conflict. It shall not authorize disclosure prohibited by other law.

Establishment provisions take effect upon enactment; operational duties begin on the implementation date specified in final regulations after resources and secure intake are available, no later than the date Congress selects in the enacted version. If a provision is held invalid, remaining provisions shall continue where they can operate consistently with the Act's purposes.
