# Founding charter for public systems assurance

PSA C01 • Discussion draft 0.1 • Proposed for Kapukai Governance Lab and adopting partners

## Purpose and commitment

We propose a publicly accessible assurance function that tests whether consequential decisions have traceable evidence and authority, safe challenge mechanisms, appropriately independent review, and verifiable correction. We will report technical findings without requiring permission from the institution examined, subject to lawful confidentiality and an agreed factual-response process.

## Scope

The common standard covers systems affecting liberty, family relationships, bodily autonomy, property control and essential services. Domain profiles may cover emergency dispatch, police and criminal justice, family and paternity proceedings, child protection, psychiatric care, adult guardianship, education, military and veterans systems, immigration, benefits and housing. Payers, vendors, researchers and contractors enter scope only where their documented functions affect a covered process.

A technical framework can span these domains. An office's legal authority cannot be assumed to do so. Each engagement shall specify the adopting instrument, covered entities, applicable law, records-access basis and exclusions. A court, executive agency and private hospital require different authority and remedy analysis.

## Allocation of responsibilities

Qualified technical reviewers determine reproducible findings within their competence. Legally authorized decision-makers determine legal consequences. Clinical judgments require relevant expertise. Affected people contribute information about participation, safety and outcomes. Technical findings do not establish criminal guilt, parental unfitness or a legal entitlement by themselves.

The originating custodian maintains or corrects its source record; each authorized decision-maker reassesses dependent actions; each recipient addresses its own records; a designated coordinator tracks completion; an independent verifier tests closure. The coordinator has no implied authority to rewrite another body's orders.

## Independence

No reviewer may assess work they designed, delivered or materially approved without disclosure and an independently approved mitigation. A material financial interest requires recusal or documented reassignment. The audited institution may correct factual errors in a draft but may not veto adverse findings. Fees shall not depend on allegations, findings, custody duration, fines or favorable conclusions.

Pilot governance shall publish appointment criteria, conflicts and funding. A standards committee shall include engineering, legal, privacy, operational and affected-person perspectives; no constituency may control a majority. Panel votes, recusals, minority opinions and amendment reasons shall be recorded. Statutory appointment arrangements require separate legislation.

# Charter operating commitments

## Accessible entry and protected participation

General audit requests may omit case identifiers. Individual review requires enough information to locate records through an authorized protected channel. The Office or pilot shall explain access, disclosure, retention and confidentiality limits before collecting sensitive information. Children shall have an appropriate representative; conflicts between a child and an adult representative require an independent representation pathway.

No participant shall be required by the pilot to publish a personal narrative, waive unrelated rights, or purchase services to request general review. Communication accommodations and a non-digital access route shall be provided within the adopted pilot scope. Absolute anonymity and reversal of existing legal duties shall not be promised.

## Findings and correction

Each finding shall identify the requirement, reviewed evidence, method, scope, uncertainty, contrary evidence and responsible function. Missing evidence is not automatic proof of misconduct. Genuine disagreement shall remain visible. Interim risk controls shall consider both continued harmful action and the risk of withdrawing needed protection or services.

An adopted correction plan shall state an owner, deadline, dependencies and acceptance evidence. Closure requires independent verification or an explicit record of why verification remains incomplete. A correction may warrant modification, reaffirmation or termination of a dependent decision; that outcome requires reasons.

## Transparency and data governance

Public reports shall separate institutional conduct from unnecessary personal identifiers, account for reidentification risk, and use lawful aggregation or redaction. Protected source records remain available only to authorized reviewers. Consent shall be granular and shall not be treated as permission to disclose other people's information. Published copies may not be recoverable; future withdrawal limits must be explained.

Records shall have defined retention periods, lawful deletion procedures and preservation holds where applicable. Audit trails shall record access and alteration without keeping unlimited copies of sensitive information. Independent security assessment is required before a pilot accepts live personal records.

## Accountability of the assurance function

Anyone affected by a technical finding may request reconsideration by a reviewer who did not make it. Confirmed errors shall be corrected with a visible version history and notice to authorized recipients. The function itself shall undergo periodic external audit. Publication shall distinguish self-assessment, peer review and independent assessment; no ISO, NIST or government endorsement is implied.
