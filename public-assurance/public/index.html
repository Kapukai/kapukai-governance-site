THE DESIGN PRINCIPLE

Connect the evidence.
Separate the conflicts.

Engineering review belongs within public accountability. It should be accessible to affected people and independent of the institution being examined. Engineers must also be accountable for their findings.

Traceable authority

Connect each consequential decision to its evidence, required conditions, responsible role and review limits.

Independent challenge

Separate review from material conflicts. Provide protected ways to question errors and participate safely.

Correction that travels

When a source changes, reassess dependent records and actions. An acknowledgment alone is not a repair.

01 / THE FRAMEWORK

A standard you can inspect.

Five control families cover the path from an assertion to action and correction. These are proposed requirements, binding only where lawfully adopted.

Evidence and authorityAA-01 to AA-05
01 · RecordPreserve source, time, provenance and changes.
02 · ClassifyKeep reports, inferences, disputes and findings distinct.
03 · Establish authorityIdentify the decision-maker and required conditions.
04 · VerifyAddress supporting and material contrary evidence.
05 · Bound actionState scope, duration and review triggers.
Participation and independenceAA-06 to AA-08
06 · Protect participationAssess safety and access barriers before avoidable disclosure or adverse labeling.
07 · Examine incentivesDocument relevant payments and conflicts; do not presume causation.
08 · Enable independent reviewDefine competence, evidence access, independence and powers.
Correction and exceptionsAA-09 to AA-12
09 · Propagate correctionIdentify and reassess decisions dependent on a changed source.
10 · Verify closureKeep incomplete work visible and assigned.
11 · Control exceptionsRecord emergency authority, limits and review.
12 · Preserve accountabilityProtect access logs, changes and retention controls.
Coupling and system coherenceAA-13 to AA-19
13 · Couple dependenciesLink actions to the evidence and authority supporting them.
14 · Decouple conflictsProtect review from interested decision-makers.
15 · Control exchangesLimit information sharing to authorized purposes.
16 · Check coherenceCompare current findings, decisions and execution.
17 · Respond to inconsistenciesAssign an owner, safeguards and a deadline.
18 · Acknowledge correctionsRecord receipt and resulting action, not receipt alone.
19 · Contain faultsDo not treat copied allegations as independent corroboration.
Federated correction and reviewAA-20 to AA-21 · proposed addendum
20 · Federated correctionAuthenticate correction notices, assign owners, preserve history, and verify dependent responses. Receipt, action and verified closure are separate states.
21 · Accountable reviewDisclose jurisdiction, appointments, funding, conflicts, evidence rules, remedies and appeals. Federation membership creates no coercive power.

Across domains: emergency response; criminal justice; family and paternity proceedings; child protection; psychiatric care; guardianship; education; military and veterans’ systems; immigration; benefits and housing. Each needs its own authority, evidence standards and review routes.

03 / Separate the powers of review

Operator

Makes the original decision. Supplies records and implements authorized corrections.

Independent reviewer

Tests evidence, implementation and conflicts. Reports methods, findings and uncertainty.

Independent verifier

Checks the correction. Does not certify its own corrective work.

Authorized appeal body

Hears challenges within its jurisdiction. Records reasons and available remedies.

An affected person can question both the original decision and the review. Public metrics show institutional performance; private evidence stays within authorized access.

TECHNICAL LEADERSHIP

Someone who speaks human and machine.

The proposed Chief Engineer for Public Systems Assurance integrates accessible communication, source-code inspection, data science, systems architecture, and electrical or control engineering. A multidisciplinary team and an independent reviewer check the work.

The test: trace a human consequence back through implemented rules, source records and institutional handoffs—and explain what the evidence does and does not establish.

03 / THE ADOPTION PATH

Start with one process.
Build evidence to expand.

We propose a first pilot of correction propagation: when a source record changes, can one participating institution identify and reassess the decisions still relying on it?

DAYS 01–15

Convene

Bring together engineering, legal, privacy, frontline and affected-person perspectives. Disclose conflicts.

DAYS 16–30

Review

Publish the draft for comment. Find a willing pilot partner and an independent evaluator.

DAYS 31–60

Test

Begin with synthetic records, then a small authorized retrospective sample. Record limits and costs.

DAYS 61–90

Revise and adopt

Publish protected findings. Resolve comments. Seek a formal institutional or legislative sponsor.

What authorizes adoption? A pilot needs a written agreement or policy approved by an official with authority over that process. Binding agency rules need an existing statutory basis and the applicable rulemaking procedure. A new federal office with compulsory powers needs legislation. State and court adoption requires their own lawful mechanisms.

Federal rulemaking petitions · 5 USC 553(e) ↗Federal standards policy ↗

This draft is not yet a consensus standard. A petition can request consideration; it cannot guarantee adoption or create new jurisdiction.

04 / RESPONSIBILITY

Independence includes
being answerable.

Technical findings and legal consequences are distinct. The proposed office would not give engineers unreviewable authority over custody, guilt, treatment or rights.
