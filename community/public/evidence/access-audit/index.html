THE SYSTEM DESIGN QUESTION / FUNCTIONS MUST MATCH AUTHORITY

Check the data. Preserve the claim. Decide the context.

A filing can establish that a claim was submitted. A successful file check does not establish that the claim is true. The audit follows provenance, authority, review, and correction across the full record lifecycle.

01 / COMPUTER

Deterministic checks

Validate file structure, required fields, signature integrity, hashes, timestamps, permissions, routing, and exact duplicates under published rules. Explain and log each result. Give people a correction path. A technically valid file is not a verified factual claim.

02 / CLERK & RECORDS

Record administration

Receive, timestamp, docket, preserve, serve, disclose or seal, and correct records as the jurisdiction’s constitution, statutes, and court rules provide. Map who appoints or elects the clerk, who supervises the work, and who controls the technology and records.

03 / AUTHORIZED HUMAN

Contextual legal judgment

Assess contested facts, credibility, relevance, legal standards, urgency, rights, and remedies through the decision-maker authorized by law. The map identifies which decisions belong to a judge or another legally authorized official at each stage.

Engineering belongs in the review loop. Engineers and technologists should test the data model, handoffs, permissions, cybersecurity, audit logs, exception paths, and correction mechanisms alongside judges, court administrators, legal experts, access advocates, and court users. Technical specialists verify system behavior; legal authority remains defined by law.

Failure mode to test: the workflow collapses “file accepted,” “technical check passed,” and “fact found”—or lets a deterministic rule trigger a context-sensitive, rights-affecting decision without accountable human review.

Map test: Can a person trace who submitted what and when, which checks ran, what remains unverified, who had authority to act, and how the record or decision can be challenged and corrected?

This is a testable system-design hypothesis, not a finding that every state uses the same clerk structure or that every court has the same defect.

SYSTEMS ENGINEERING VIEW / EVENT-DRIVEN, ASYNCHRONOUS

Track the case and the evidence on separate lanes.

Court events arrive over time from different people and systems. Model the workflow as an asynchronous state machine: every submission, service event, response, correction, and decision is timestamped, attributed, and traceable. The case’s procedural state and each claim’s evidence status move in parallel; neither lane silently proves the other.

RECEIVED ≠ INTEGRITY-CHECKED ≠ EVIDENCE-ASSESSED ≠ FACT-FOUND

Computers can answer bounded questions

  • Was a file attached to this submission?
  • Does its hash or digital signature match a defined reference?
  • Are required fields present, and which rule version ran?
  • Were actor, event time, case identifier, and system result logged?

Return a defined result such as PASS, FAIL, UNKNOWN, or EXCEPTION. A failure should identify the check and a correction path; it is not a finding about truth or the merits.

People must decide contextual questions

  • Does an item support a particular factual proposition?
  • Is it admissible, reliable, relevant, or outweighed?
  • How do competing accounts, legal standards, urgency, and rights apply?
  • What finding or remedy is legally authorized, and what reasons support it?

These judgments depend on evidence, procedure, context, and the law. A technical check can inform them; it cannot make them.

High-signal public label: “Allegation filed; evidence status and court finding shown separately.” A narrative becomes traceable when each material proposition links to its supporting and contrary sources, provenance, challenge status, and any authorized finding. “No exhibit attached to this packet” means exactly that; it does not prove no evidence exists elsewhere.

This is not one rigid linear pipeline. Parties may submit at different times; service, responses, emergencies, amendments, and appeals can branch or repeat. Preserve the event sequence, causal links, actor and authority, applicable rule version, and every status change. Access to file remains a legal question; a machine check is not a truth gate.

CONSTITUTIONAL BASELINE / A NECESSARY CORRECTION

The founding-era story is not one uniform rule.

The U.S. Constitution vests federal judicial power in courts; Article III does not prescribe a nationwide clerk–judge structure. The Judiciary Act of 1789 authorized federal judges to appoint clerks, assigned them recordkeeping duties, and clerks also screened filings for compliance with law and court rules. That is a distinct administrative role within the judiciary—not proof that every state constitution adopted one identical separation.

State arrangements differ. Florida’s constitution provides for a clerk in each county and ties selection to its county-officer provisions; it also allows clerk duties to be divided. New York generally elects county clerks, while its constitution assigns appointment and removal of New York City’s county clerks to the Appellate Division. The map will record each state’s constitutional text, enabling statutes, court rules, and actual administration separately.

02 / How a map label earns its place

A finding carries its scope.

Each entry must identify its source level—constitution, statute, court rule, local procedure, system record, or observed operation—and name the jurisdiction, court type, procedure, time period, and evidence. Ratings describe what the sources establish within that scope; they are not a general grade for a state, court, judge, or party.

A

Documented failure

Authoritative evidence establishes a specific failure within the stated scope.

B

Safeguard documented in operation

Evidence shows a specific safeguard functioning within the stated scope.

C

Mixed findings

Documented safeguards and documented failures coexist within the scope.

D

Insufficient evidence

The defined research is complete, but available evidence cannot support a reliable determination.

E

Not yet assessed

Research for the defined jurisdiction and procedure is not complete.

Severity and evidence scope stay separate

A rating alone never proves that a failure is systemic. The public label must also say whether the evidence supports a system-level finding, a recurring pattern, a single documented case, or only a rule or official instruction. Only an assessed system-level finding receives the label “Systemic.” A single case stays case-level.

  1. SUBMITTEDClaim, source, author, time
  2. CHECKEDIntegrity and rule-based fields
  3. RECORDEDClerk or authorized intake
  4. CHALLENGEDNotice and evidence access
  5. DECIDEDAuthorized human applies law
  6. REVIEWEDService, correction, appeal

This lifecycle is an analytical aid, not a claim that every procedure follows the same sequence. Filing access remains distinct from factual verification. Constitutional text, rules, actual system behavior, individual cases, recurring patterns, and systemic findings are recorded separately.
