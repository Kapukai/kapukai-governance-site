02 / How a map label earns its place

A finding carries its scope.

Each rating must name the jurisdiction, court type, procedure, time period, and evidence. The classification describes what the sources establish within that scope. It is not a general grade for a state, court, judge, or party.

A

Documented failure

Authoritative evidence establishes a specific failure within the stated scope.

B

Safeguard documented in operation

Evidence shows a specific safeguard functioning within the stated scope.

C

Mixed findings

Documented safeguards and documented failures coexist within the scope.

D

Insufficient evidence

The defined research is complete, but available evidence cannot support a reliable determination.

E

Not yet assessed

Research for the defined jurisdiction and procedure is not complete.

Severity and evidence scope stay separate

A rating alone never proves that a failure is systemic. The public label must also say whether the evidence supports a system-level finding, a recurring pattern, a single documented case, or only a rule or official instruction. Only an assessed system-level finding receives the label “Systemic.” A single case stays case-level.

  1. ASSERTEDClaim or filing
  2. EXAMINEDEvidence offered and challenged
  3. DETERMINEDFinding within the court’s authority
  4. ENFORCEDOrder, handoff, or correction

This four-stage model is an analytical aid, not a claim that every procedure follows the same sequence. Law on the books, published instructions, actual operations, one incident, recurring patterns, and systemic findings are recorded separately.
