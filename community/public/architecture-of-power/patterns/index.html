The question that connects the kit

Can a system prevent and correct harm as reliably as it exercises power?

Follow the consequence. Trace the correction. Identify who can act, what evidence they need, and how anyone knows the repair worked.

One pattern. One test. A visible result.

1. Choose a question.
Start with the part of the process you cannot explain.

2. Map the real workflow.
Add sources, authority, owners, versions and timestamps.

3. Test a repair.
Measure burden, protection and recurrence together.

The practical “hack” is the inspection question: it helps you locate missing evidence or responsibility. Each repair remains a proposal until tested.

P01 / Information / Conceptual pattern

Correction must reach the consequence

A correction may arrive at the source while consequences continue wherever the earlier information traveled.

Recognize
Trace one accepted correction through every known recipient that used the earlier information.
Inspect
Recipient lists, correction decisions, delivery acknowledgments, updated records, and evidence that downstream restrictions changed.
Measure
Compare recipients updated, restrictions removed, and elapsed time with the original information’s reach.
Repair to test
Assign correction owners, confirm recipient updates, and check whether downstream consequences actually end.

Keep the tradeoff visible

Fast propagation helps timely action; it also needs reliable correction paths and limits on unnecessary sharing.

Check another explanation: Every affected recipient updates promptly, and verified consequences end without the obsolete information reappearing.

Sources and limits of the claim

P02 / Responsibility / Conceptual pattern

The unowned handoff

A referral can move the paperwork without transferring responsibility for the next decision.

Recognize
At each transfer, identify who accepts the task and who can resolve a stalled handoff.
Inspect
Transfer logs, acceptance records, delegated authority, escalation rules, and elapsed time between responsible owners.
Measure
Count transfers without acknowledgment, unresolved ownership disputes, and time spent waiting between offices.
Repair to test
Name the receiving owner, require acceptance, and define escalation when responsibility remains disputed.

Keep the tradeoff visible

Specialization can improve decisions, but additional handoffs need explicit responsibility and enough context.

Check another explanation: A referral is accepted by a named owner who has authority and resources to resolve it.

P03 / Information / Conceptual pattern

Copies are not independent corroboration

Several matching records may repeat one assertion rather than provide several independent reasons to believe it.

Recognize
Follow each supporting record back to its originating evidence and later transformations.
Inspect
Original observations, source identifiers, derivation history, timestamps, and reasons for treating sources as independent.
Measure
Count independent source observations separately from copies, summaries, and records derived from the same source.
Repair to test
Preserve provenance and distinguish independent support from repeated claims before weighing the evidence.

Keep the tradeoff visible

Sharing improves access, but repetition must not be treated as additional independent evidence.

Check another explanation: Matching records come from separate observations with independent evidentiary support.

Sources and limits of the claim

  • W3C PROV · origins and derivationProvides a framework for recording sources, agents, processing, versioning, and derivation; the corroboration test is our application.

P04 / Information / Conceptual pattern

Labels outrun their evidence

A tentative label can acquire greater authority as it travels even when its supporting evidence has not changed.

Recognize
Track where an allegation, provisional assessment, or confirmed finding changes category or triggers a stronger response.
Inspect
Original wording, status changes, supporting evidence, applicable criteria, authorizations, and triggered actions.
Measure
Count escalations without a traceable evidentiary basis, applicable rule, or authorized review.
Repair to test
Keep allegations, assessments, and findings distinct; require evidence and authorized review before changing status.

Keep the tradeoff visible

Early warnings can support protection; provisional status and timely review help limit unsupported consequences.

Check another explanation: A stronger classification follows new evidence and documented review under the applicable rule.

P05 / Responsibility / Conceptual pattern

Closed ticket, unresolved consequence

Closing a complaint or completing a task does not, by itself, show that the underlying problem was repaired.

Recognize
Check the affected person’s situation after administrative closure, including any continuing restrictions.
Inspect
Closure criteria, follow-up records, restored access, remaining restrictions, and recurrence checks.
Measure
Report closure separately from verified repair, time to relief, and recurrence of the same problem.
Repair to test
Define the outcome required for resolution, verify it, and reopen cases when the problem persists.

Keep the tradeoff visible

Outcome verification takes resources; prioritize serious consequences and avoid making affected people repeatedly prove the same unresolved harm.

Check another explanation: Closure follows verified repair, and later monitoring finds no continuing consequence from the original error.

Sources and limits of the claim

P06 / Incentives / Conceptual pattern

Less burden. Effective protection.

A procedural step should be judged by the protection it provides alongside the effort and delay it imposes.

Recognize
Ask what risk each requirement addresses and who bears its time, cost, and accessibility demands.
Inspect
Requirement purpose, completion and abandonment data, user effort, accessibility evidence, and measured protection.
Measure
Compare errors prevented with completion time, repeated submissions, abandonment, and unequal access.
Repair to test
Remove duplication, offer accessible routes, and retain safeguards that demonstrably protect rights or public interests.

Keep the tradeoff visible

Simplification can remove friction or remove protection; assess both before changing a requirement.

Check another explanation: A demanding requirement demonstrably prevents serious harm, with accessible assistance and proportionate exceptions.

Sources and limits of the claim

These references support the design principle or a scoped example. The card itself is a conceptual illustration, not a finding about every institution.

  • OECD · regulatory policy and governanceRecommends reducing unnecessary burdens while preserving legitimate objectives and evaluating public outcomes.
  • OECD · public service rightsConnects service design with proportionality, privacy, procedural guarantees, review, and timely remedy. Supports lawful, proportionate administration, independent review, and timely remedy; this card asks who can lawfully provide interim protection without asserting a universal pause power.

P07 / Responsibility / Conceptual pattern

Independent review, connected repair

Independent review needs connected repair and a clearly assigned, lawful route to interim protection where available.

Recognize
Who has lawful authority to pause harmful action pending review, under what conditions and response time?
Inspect
Governing authority, pause criteria, request logs, reasons, review deadlines, and evidence of downstream implementation.
Measure
Track pause requests, response times, review dates, justified refusals, and whether authorized relief reaches every responsible office.
Repair to test
Preserve independent review; define lawful pause conditions, deadlines, review, and connected repair without unnecessary data sharing.

Keep the tradeoff visible

A pause can prevent worsening harm or interrupt needed protection; define proportionate conditions and prompt review.

Check another explanation: A boundary preserves impartial review while a separately authorized official promptly implements temporary relief.

Sources and limits of the claim

P08 / Incentives / Conceptual pattern

Metrics shape choices

Targets can direct attention toward fast completion while leaving accuracy, access, or lasting repair less visible.

Recognize
Compare rewarded targets with the outcomes the service is supposed to deliver.
Inspect
Target definitions, performance reviews, workload data, validation samples, outcome trends, and documented responses to missed targets.
Measure
Review speed and volume alongside error rates, unresolved harm, repeat work, and access across groups.
Repair to test
Balance output targets with outcome checks, review workload pressures, and investigate unexpected changes in reported performance.

Keep the tradeoff visible

More measures can reveal blind spots but increase reporting work; keep the set small and decision-relevant.

Check another explanation: Balanced measures reward accurate, timely outcomes, and independent checks confirm that reported improvements persist.

A library that can learn

Keep the pattern separate from the finding.

What kind of claim?

Conceptual illustration · proposed test · documented case finding · cross-case finding.

What is its evidence status?

Unassessed · supported within a stated scope and date · disputed · superseded.

All eight cards begin as conceptual patterns with proposed tests. An official case report can establish a specific occurrence. It does not establish prevalence, a national ranking or coordinated intent. Changes in wording, evidence or diagrams receive a new version.

Keep the collection selective. Add a card only when it explains a distinct mechanism, changes what someone would inspect, and offers a repair that can be tested. Put new examples of an existing mechanism under the existing card. Download the new-pattern template.

Choose the right visual for the question
  • Where does an error spread? Use the provenance and correction networks: P01, P03 and P04.
  • Who owns the next action? Use the handoff and review maps: P02 and P07.
  • Did the fix reach the person? Use the outcome chain: P05.
  • Which requirements can we simplify? Use the burden/protection matrix: P06, alongside the article’s dependency map.
  • What behavior does the system encourage? Use the measurement feedback loop: P08.
