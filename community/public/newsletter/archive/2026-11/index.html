Where did the correction go?

November month-to-date issue · November 1–5, 2026 · Published November 5, 2026

Suppose a letter says you missed an appointment. The office checks its attendance sheet, finds your signature, and corrects its file. A second office still has the old letter. Has the decision that relied on it changed?

This is a fictional example, but it gives an organizer, engineer or reviewer something specific to look for. A confirmation from the first office establishes that someone amended the source. You still need a way to see which other records carried the error and who can reconsider the decision.

Try a correction trace

  1. Identify the source.
    Write down the dated record and the exact statement being corrected. Keep a reference to both versions.
  2. Follow one copy.
    Identify one recipient who relied on the earlier version. Record when that recipient acknowledged receiving the correction.
  3. Check the outcome.
    Ask the person responsible for the decision to record whether it changed, the reason, and how the affected person can request another review.

You can run the exercise with a made-up record in the public accountability demonstration. The map and records are fictional; their counts do not measure real-world prevalence.
