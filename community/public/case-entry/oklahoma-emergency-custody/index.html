Threat model / testable hypothesis

Could unsupported or misattributed information enter an official record and later be treated as verified?

This is the question to test, not a finding that Oklahoma has a broken control or that any person filed a false report. The public sources establish legal pathways and required decisions. They do not reveal whether production systems preserve source provenance, prevent alteration, or enforce every required handoff.

Scope: this is a statutory process and control map for child emergency custody. It is separate from the neighboring OUCMS civil e-filing pilot. No child-welfare portal, case file, or individual case has been accessed or tested.

01 / The entry paths

There is more than one way in.

Oklahoma law describes distinct prepetition paths. A public audit must map each path before drawing conclusions about a form or portal.

PATH A / PROTECTIVE CUSTODY

Officer or court employee

A peace officer or court employee may take a child into custody without a court order when the statutory reasonable-suspicion and imminent-safety conditions are met.

Oklahoma Title 10A, § 1-4-201(A)(1), p. 74

PATH B / COURT ORDER

District attorney application

A district court may issue an order on a district attorney’s application. A supporting sworn affidavit may be based on information and belief; the application must still state facts sufficient for the court to assess the statutory reasonable-suspicion and imminent-safety criteria.

Oklahoma Title 10A, § 1-4-201(A)(2), p. 74

PATH C / TREATMENT ORDER

Emergency medical or behavioral health care

A separate court-order path applies when treatment is needed to protect the child and a parent or custodian is unwilling or unavailable to consent. The order must specify the authorized evaluation or treatment.

Oklahoma Title 10A, § 1-4-201(A)(3), p. 74

Important distinction: “information and belief” is expressly permitted for the supporting affidavit in Path B. The test is whether the source, basis, author, timing, and required factual assertions remain traceable—not whether every emergency application must arrive with trial-admissible evidence attachments.

02 / Statutory clocks and state changes

Follow each event to its required next record.

A deadline is testable only when the triggering event and the required record can be compared.

1

Protective custody begins

For the officer route, the law provides for a joint-response safety evaluation. At a reception center, the evaluation protocol is due within 23 hours; if the evaluation finds no imminent safety threat, the child must be restored. For this route, the Department’s emergency-custody status requires the evaluation, an imminent-threat conclusion, and a court order.

Oklahoma Title 10A, § 1-4-201(B), pp. 75–76

2

Emergency court review

The court must hold an emergency custody hearing within the next two judicial days. It may receive oral or written reports, affidavits, or testimony; information with probative value may be received even if it would not be admissible under the Oklahoma Evidence Code. The court must determine whether facts establish the statutory reasonable-suspicion and immediate-protection criteria.

Oklahoma Title 10A, § 1-4-203(A), p. 79

3

Petition or expiration

A deprived-child petition is generally due within seven judicial days of custody. A limited extension may be granted for compelling reasons; if the petition is not timely filed, the emergency order expires and the district attorney must file a record stating why no petition was filed and to whom the child was released.

Oklahoma Title 10A, § 1-4-205(B), p. 84

4

Adjudication is a separate decision

The later adjudication hearing has its own deadline and proof rules. A nonjury finding that a child is deprived must be based on sworn testimony and the statutory preponderance standard. An emergency-custody entry or receipt is not that adjudication.

Oklahoma Title 10A, §§ 1-4-601–602, pp. 104–105

03 / Follow authority across branches

One entry can cross three branches.

Map the handoff, not just the agency name. Keep the record event distinct from the legal decision.

01 / LEGISLATURE

Defines authority and conditions

Oklahoma Title 10A defines who may act, the emergency criteria, required notices, hearings, findings, and time limits. Congress defines federal Title IV-E eligibility and payment rules.

02 / EXECUTIVE

Reports, applies, investigates, acts, and claims

Law enforcement, district attorneys, and the child-welfare agency carry out separate steps. The audit asks who supplied each assertion, who evaluated it, what record was created, and what eligible expenditure was later claimed.

03 / JUDICIARY

Receives records and makes findings

The clerk’s receipt or docket entry records a filing event. A judge’s order or adjudication is a separate legal event. The map tests whether each handoff and finding can be traced to its supporting record.

Funding link / conditional, not automatic

Trace eligibility and actual expenditures; do not treat case creation as a payment event.

Federal Title IV-E foster-care maintenance eligibility includes statutory requirements such as an eligible child and placement, state-agency responsibility, and a judicial determination that remaining home would be contrary to the child’s welfare and that reasonable efforts were made. Federal payments are calculated from qualifying expenditures. A case opening, filing receipt, or custody allegation alone does not establish that a payment was claimed or received.

42 U.S.C. § 672(a)(1)–(2) · 42 U.S.C. § 674(a)(1)
