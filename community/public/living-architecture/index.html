A system can function.
And still fail the people it serves.

What if the measure of an institution were not merely the cases it processes or the money it spends—but what actually happens to people?

The Living Architecture Model traces human need, legal authority, funding, information, decisions, outcomes, and correction. Use it to frame an evidence-based question—not to presume wrongdoing.

A REPRODUCIBLE APPROACH

Seven links to examine.

  1. Human needDefine the affected population, need, rights, and plausible harms.
  2. Legal authorityRecord jurisdiction, decision rights, law, and independent review.
  3. FundingTrace authorizations, allocations, spending, and incentives separately.
  4. Information inputDistinguish allegations, verified findings, uncertainties and disputed evidence.
  5. Decision + handoffIdentify who decided, what was relied upon, and downstream actions.
  6. Human outcomeMeasure benefits, harms, different groups, and uncertainty.
  7. Correction + learningTest challenge, remedy, propagation, and redesign.

A missing arrow is a research question, not proof of capture, motive, unlawful conduct, or causation.

Living Architecture v1.1 assessment questions
IDControl pointTest question
A01JustificationIs the human need and intended population documented?
A02ParticipationWere affected people involved before requirements were fixed?
A03AuthorizationIs the decision-maker's authority documented and current?
A04Funding authorizationIs the legal source and purpose of funds traceable?
A05Funding allocationCan spending be linked to activities and intended outcomes?
A06Input provenanceAre claims identified separately from verified facts?
A07Evidence intakeCan contrary evidence enter the record with a receipt?
A08Independent reviewCan a qualified independent reviewer examine a challenge?
A09Decision recordAre reasons, authority, and timestamps preserved?
A10Execution handoffCan downstream actions be traced to an authorized decision?
A11Human outcomeAre intended and unintended human outcomes measured?
A12PrivacyCan decisions be audited without exposing personal data?
A13CorrectionCan an error be corrected with a documented deadline?
A14PropagationDo corrections reach downstream systems and affected people?
A15Learning / retirementDo findings change design, funding, or retirement decisions?

Use Pass, Fail, Unknown, or Not applicable with supporting evidence. Do not sum to a universal rating: the instrument is not validated for cross-institution rankings.

SOURCES & LIMITATIONS

Inspect the foundations.

Kapukai's model is an original proposed synthesis, not a certification issued by the organizations below. Their frameworks have narrower scopes and should not be presented as endorsing Kapukai.

  • ISO 9241-210:2019 — Human-centred design principles for interactive systems; confirmed current in 2025.
  • CDC Program Evaluation Framework (2024) — Program evaluation with rigor, utility, independence, transparency, and ethics.
  • NIST AI RMF 1.0 (2023) — Voluntary AI-specific risk-management framework; revision work ongoing in 2026.

Interpretations of law, admissibility, causality, funding incentives, and rights require jurisdiction-specific primary evidence and appropriate independent review. The tool does not diagnose misconduct and is not legal advice or a validated expert opinion.
